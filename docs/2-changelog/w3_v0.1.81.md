## Changes

This release implements slice-0 ticket 26 under the plan `docs/1-plans/backup-restore-mutual-exclusion.plan.md`, on the triage's rulings (a)–(e) of 2026-09-17. Neither `backup run` nor `restore` looked for the other, so the 01:00 nightly and a restore could run at once: the nightly failed when the restore's `stop` stage took Postgres down, and its `.partial` directory was then exactly the staging `v0.1.78`'s fresh-stack rule classifies as incomplete. `backup run`, `backup push`, and `restore` now share one lock, and a second started while one runs refuses before any stage.

- **The seam's lock pair** (`gideon/host/sysio.py`). A second protocol, `LockingHost(Host, Protocol)`, adds `take_lock(path, record) -> str | None` and `release_lock(path)`; `RealHost` implements them over an exclusive non-blocking `fcntl.flock`, the file created at 0600, the descriptor non-inheritable and kept open in a dict keyed by path, the record written through it on success and read back through it on refusal. Release closes the descriptor and never unlinks the file, since an unlink races a taker holding the old inode. An extension protocol rather than two members on `Host`, because a member added to `Host` fails mypy across 60 files of fakes (480 errors, measured in the cycle).
- **The lock module** (`gideon/host/backuplock.py`, new). The release constants `/run/gideon` (0700, created by the taker) and `/run/gideon/backup.lock` — tmpfs, outside the staging directory `swap` moves (ruling d); a frozen `Record` of command, pid, and the run's clock as one JSON line, parsed tolerantly; `take(io, command=, now=)` returning `HELD`, `NESTED` (the record's pid is this process's: the holder's own in-process child, which passes and releases nothing), or `REFUSED` with a `report.Problem` naming the holder, its start instant, and its pid, the fix to wait and check `ps -p <pid>` (an empty or malformed record refuses with the unreadable-record wording); `release(io)`.
- **The three entries** (`backup.py`, `restore.py`). `run_backup_run`, `run_backup_push`, and `run_restore` take the lock after their root and clock checks and before their preconditions: refused prints the module's `_refuse` on stderr and returns 1 with no stage row; held runs the body inside `try/finally` releasing; nested runs the body alone. Each entry's body moved whole into `_backup_run_body`, `_backup_push_body`, and `_restore_body`; no stage, row, or refusal of the bodies changed. `restore`'s pre-restore run and target push are its nested runs and pass (ruling c).
- **The in-process callers** (`install.py`, `upgrade.py`). Typing only: `run_install`, `run_upgrade`, and the `_default_runners`/`_run_rollback` path take a `LockingHost`, so `install`'s `backup` phase and `upgrade`'s and `--rollback`'s safety runs take and release the lock around their in-process `backup run` and hold nothing across their stages; the rollback's `restore` child takes it in its own process; the drill takes none.
- **The nightly refuses rather than declines** (ruling b). A refused `backup run` exits 1, `gideon-backup.service` is `failed`, the push never starts, and the existing "Backup unit failed" alert pages, the unit's journal carrying the line that names the holder. No unit, rule, or rendered file changed.
- **Tests.** The fakes of `tests/test_backup.py`, `test_backup_push.py`, and `test_restore.py` carry the lock pair over a dict of held paths; each command is proven refused under a seeded foreign holder (stderr naming it, exit 1, no stage, no command run), taking and releasing the lock, and releasing it on a failed stage; `restore` holds it while its pre-restore run and target push pass as nested. `tests/test_backuplock.py` (new) holds the record's round trip and tolerant parse, the three outcomes, and `RealHost`'s pair over a temporary directory. `test_install.py` and `test_upgrade.py` are unchanged and green. The gate is green: `ruff`, `mypy` (208 files), and the suite.

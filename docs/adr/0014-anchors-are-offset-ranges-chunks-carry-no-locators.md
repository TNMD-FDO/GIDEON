# Anchors are offset ranges; chunks carry no locators

Every locator a citation can name — a reporter's star page, a PDF page, a Bates stamp, a transcript volume/page/line, a US Code identifier, a Guidelines section — is a row in `anchors(doc_id, kind, label, char_start, char_end, attrs)`: a labelled range of code-point offsets over the document's canonical text (ADR-0013). A chunk stores no page, Bates number, or line of its own; its locators are derived by intersecting its offsets with the anchors, a pincite check is "does the quoted span intersect the anchor with that label", and a chunk with no anchor of the kind a citation needs is explicitly **un-pinciteable** rather than guessed at. Several rows of one kind may cover one logical unit: Harvard CAP's `pgmap` attribute records a single paragraph straddling two print pages (`pgmap="14(130) 15(120)"`), and federal transcripts fix 25 lines to a page regardless of where speech breaks, so a scalar column would have to pick one page and lose the rest. Greenfield-spec ticket 13, Aug 2026; evidence in `docs/research/chunk-addressing-and-dedupe.md` (branch `research/chunk-addressing-and-dedupe`).

**Rejected:** locator columns on the chunk (`page_no`, `bates`, `line_start`) — cannot hold compound pagination, and every new locator kind is a schema change plus a reprocess of every document; page-bounded chunking (a chunk never crosses a page) — lets the print layout, not the argument, cut the retrieval unit; a provenance tree serialized into the index (LlamaIndex nodes, Docling `DocItem.prov` lists) as the record — the same row/offset shape, but the record is Postgres, not a tree in a derived store.

**Consequences:** the parser must extract locators into anchors at ingestion (CAP `pgmap`, PDF page breaks, Bates stamps, the 25-line transcript grid, USLM identifiers) and `gideon index build` reports anchor coverage per collection; pincite precision (release gate 1.00) and pincite coverage become measurable per generation; the Review and Transcribe branches add anchor kinds, never columns, and never reprocess; a denormalized "starting page" may be materialized as a read optimization on top of the table, never instead of it.
